title: The Plates

In 1888 Walter Buller, a Wellington lawyer and New Zealand's most famous ornithologist, published the second edition of *A History of the Birds of New Zealand*, illustrated with hand-coloured lithographs by the Dutch artist J. G. Keulemans, who lived and worked in England. They are some of the most beautiful bird pictures ever printed, and several images in this issue, including the cover, come from them.

They are also a record of a catastrophe, made by people who were part of it. Victorian collectors widely assumed that New Zealand's birds were doomed to be replaced by "more vigorous" European species, and concluded that the sensible thing was to get good specimens before they went. Buller himself collected eighteen huia on one expedition in 1883. In his *History* he describes a pair of huia "caressing each other with their beautiful bills" in a tree overhead, until "a charge of No. 6 brought them both to the ground together". He felt, he wrote, "almost glad that the shot was not mine, although by no means loth to appropriate 2 fine specimens".

Of the birds on these two pages, the morepork, on the left of the opposite page, is still calling in the bush at night. Its larger cousin, the whēkau or laughing owl, was gone by about 1914. Both piopio, the North and South Island thrushes, were last confirmed around the turn of the twentieth century. The pictures outlived them.

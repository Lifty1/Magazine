kicker: From the editor
title: Presumed
---

Every species in this magazine was, at some point, written off. Not carelessly: by serious people, on reasonable evidence, after searches that came back empty. The takahē was gone. The coelacanth had been gone for sixty-six million years. The cahow had been a word in old documents for three centuries. And then, one day, somebody found one.

These are among the best stories in natural history, and they all have the same shape. Something is lost. People stop looking, except for one or two who don't. There is a footprint, a pile of droppings, a fish on a trawler deck, a bird in a lighthouse. And then the long, slow, unglamorous work of keeping the thing that came back.

Biologists have a name for a species that disappears from the record and turns up again. They call it a *Lazarus taxon*, after the man in the Gospel of John who had been dead four days when he was called out of his tomb. It seemed like the right name for a magazine about second chances that nobody was owed.

I have tried not to make this a magazine of miracles. Rediscoveries are rare. Most lost species are not hiding; they are gone, and a few pages near the back are given to some of those, and to the people still searching for them anyway. The essay in the middle is about the newest idea in this field, that if something cannot be found it might instead be made, and why I think that idea, however clever, misreads what these stories teach.

What they teach is not that extinction is reversible. It is that we are worse at knowing what is alive than we think, that the people who find things are usually the ones who were paying attention to what everyone else ignored, and that finding is the beginning, not the end.

This first issue has a print run of one. It was researched, written and designed by Claude, an AI made by Anthropic, working from published sources, every one of which is listed at the back. The pictures are public-domain or openly licensed, and their makers are credited. Where the record is uncertain, I have tried to say so.

Read it slowly. Some of these animals took a very long time to be found.
